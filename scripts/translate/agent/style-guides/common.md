## Rules for every language

### Meaning

1. Translate the meaning of each sentence completely. Do not add, explain, soften, or leave out anything.
2. Keep the sentence boundaries of the English. One English sentence becomes one sentence. The audio and the sentence time maps depend on this.
3. Keep the paragraph as one unit. Do not move content between paragraphs.
4. Do not add notes, comments, or headings.
5. When a word in your translation can also be read in a sense that the authors did not intend, choose another word. The reader sees only your words.

### Tone

1. Write a clear, dignified, modern text. A serious adult reader must find it natural.
2. Do not use archaic words or forms. Do not use casual words.
3. Keep the long sentences and the dense ideas of the English. Make them clear with good word order and punctuation, not by cutting them.
4. Translate the text. Do not judge it. Do not add or remove words that show belief or doubt.

### Terms

1. Use the required terms from the glossary of the paper.
2. Keep the capitals of the English for the coined terms: "Universal Father", "Thought Adjuster", "Ancients of Days". Do not capitalize other words because they seem important.
3. Keep "Urantia" as "Urantia". Keep every name that the glossary keeps the same.
4. When a coined term is not in the glossary, choose one form and use it in the full paper. Record it in `newTerms` in the draft: `{ "English term": "your form" }`.
5. When you are not sure what a term means, look it up with the urantia.dev MCP server, in English only: `entities_get`, `search_semantic`, or `paragraphs_context`. Do not guess.

### Quotes

1. The book quotes the Bible in its own words, and it often changes the wording. Translate the book's English words. Do not put in a published Bible translation.
2. Use the quotation marks of the target language. Keep the quote at the same place in the sentence.

### Form

1. Keep every HTML tag and class exactly. Translate only the text between the tags. Keep `<em>` on the word that matches the English emphasis.
2. Keep each number in digits when the English uses digits. Keep references like "2:0.1" as they are.
3. The first word of a paper is in capitals in the English ("THE Universal Father"). Do the same with the first word of the translation.
