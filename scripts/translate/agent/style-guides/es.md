## Spanish rules

### Variety

1. Write neutral Spanish that a reader in Mexico, Colombia, Argentina, or Spain reads without effort. Most readers are in Latin America.
2. Do not use words or forms of only one country.
3. Use "ustedes" for the plural "you". Do not use "vosotros" or its verb forms: write "piensen", not "pensad".
4. When the text speaks to the reader, use the plural "ustedes", or an impersonal form when that reads better.
5. When Jesus or another person speaks to one person, use "tú". When the English shows great respect to one person (a ruler, a judge), use "usted".

### Pronouns for God

1. The English writes "he", "him", and "his" for God in lowercase. Write "él", "lo", and "su" in lowercase. Do not add capitals that the English does not have.

### Words

1. "paper" (a part of the book) is "documento". "Paper 1" is "Documento 1".
2. "mankind" and "man" in the general sense: "la humanidad" and "el hombre", as the English does. Do not change the wording to make it inclusive.
3. Avoid false friends: "actual" is "real" or "efectivo", "eventually" is "finalmente" or "con el tiempo", "realize" is "darse cuenta" or "llevar a cabo".
4. Keep the "-ción" and "-dad" nouns to a natural number. When a verb reads better than a noun, use the verb.

### Punctuation and numbers

1. Use « » for quotes, and “ ” for a quote inside a quote.
2. Use the opening marks ¿ and ¡.
3. For a parenthetical phrase, use the long dash — without spaces, as in the English, or commas when they read better.
4. Numbers: no separator in a number of four digits (1934). For five digits or more, use a space as the thousands separator (200 000), as the RAE recommends. Use a comma for decimals.
5. Keep the English capitals for coined terms. Use lowercase for days, months, languages, and nationalities, as Spanish requires.
