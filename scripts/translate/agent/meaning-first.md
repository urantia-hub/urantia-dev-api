## Method: meaning first

Translate what the authors meant, not the English words. Do these steps in order.

1. **Find the meaning in English.** Find where the papers define or explain the term or phrase. The Foreword (Paper 0) defines many terms on purpose: Deity, divinity, God, reality, personality, mind, spirit, the Supreme, the Ultimate, the Absolutes, and the universes. When the Foreword defines a term, start there. Then read two or three uses in other papers, because some words change sense with the context. Use the urantia.dev MCP server, in English only: `search_fulltext` for the term (for example, "midway between"), `paragraphs_get` or `paragraphs_context` for the passage, and `entities_get` for the entity. A description in this packet is a summary, not the source. The papers are the source.
2. **State the meaning in one sentence**, with the reference that shows it. Example: "midwayers: beings on a level of life between mortals and angels (77:0.1, 77:8.4)".
3. **Rate your confidence** that you know the English meaning: `high` (the papers state it), `medium` (the papers imply it), or `low` (you are not sure). Do not hide a doubt. A `low` rating is useful, because it sends the term to a human.
4. **Find candidate forms** in the target language that carry that meaning.
5. **Test each candidate for an accidental meaning:**
   - another common sense of the word (Spanish "intermedio" as a noun is an intermission)
   - a false friend (Spanish "actual" means "current", not "real")
   - a sense that changes the idea (a go-between is not the same as a being between two levels)
   - a religious or cultural sense that the authors did not intend
   - the same form as another term in the glossary
6. **Choose the form that is most truthful to what the authors meant.** When the English coins a word, prefer a form that reads as a term. When the English uses a plain word, use a plain word. Then rate your confidence in the form separately from the meaning.
7. **Record the forms that you rejected**, with one short reason each. A reviewer learns the most from this list.

### Do not close what the authors left open

Translate the meaning that the English gives, no more. When a sentence can be read in two ways in English and the context does not decide, keep both readings open in the translation. Do not choose the reading that you think is more likely. That is commentary, not translation.

The target language sometimes forces a choice that English does not. In Spanish, for example: "ser" or "estar", the gender of a noun or a pronoun, "tú" or "usted", the mood of a verb. When it forces a choice, choose the reading that the context supports best, and record it in `forcedChoices` with one short reason.
